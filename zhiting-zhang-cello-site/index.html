01 — Why Study With Me

Every student
learns differently.

My teaching combines strong technical foundations with musical understanding, helping students not only play the cello well, but understand how and why they play.

Lessons are individually designed to help students:

  • Build a natural, healthy, and reliable technique
  • Develop a beautiful and expressive sound
  • Practice more independently and effectively
  • Gain confidence in lessons, auditions, and performances
  • Discover their own musical voice

Whether a student is learning for personal enjoyment or preparing for auditions, competitions, and advanced ensembles, my goal is to help them grow with confidence, curiosity, and purpose.

02 — Meet Zhiting

Cellist · Educator

Thoughtful, confident,
independent musicians.

Zhiting Zhang is a cellist and educator dedicated to developing thoughtful, confident, and independent musicians.

She received her Master of Music degree from the Cleveland Institute of Music, where she studied with Richard Weiss of The Cleveland Orchestra. Her performance experience spans solo, chamber, and orchestral settings, with appearances at venues including Benaroya Hall and Severance Music Center.

Her teaching brings together healthy technique, musical sensitivity, and expressive freedom. Students are guided to understand not only how to play, but how to listen, think, and make informed musical choices of their own.

Her goal is to give each student the technical foundation, musical understanding, and confidence to develop an individual voice—both on the cello and as a musician.

Zhiting is a member of the Music Teachers National Association (MTNA).

03 — Teaching Philosophy

More Than
Technique.

I believe learning an instrument is about more than playing the right notes. Through cello study, students learn to listen deeply, express ideas with confidence, develop musical sensitivity, and become more aware of how their bodies move.

Technique should never feel separate from music. I help students develop a natural and efficient relationship with the instrument so that physical freedom supports musical expression—rather than getting in its way.

My goal is for students to need fewer instructions, not more: to hear what they want, understand how to achieve it, and develop the confidence to make their own musical decisions.
